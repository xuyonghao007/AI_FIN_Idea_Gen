Honoring Michael C. Jensen

Michael C. Jensen died on April 2, 2024 at age 84. He founded the 
Journal of Financial Economics in 1974 in partnership with Eugene Fama 
and Robert C. Merton. Jensen was the managing editor of the Journal 
from 1974 to 1997. He quickly built the Journal into what it has been for 
many decades, namely a journal that has constantly been ranked as one 
of the top journals in financial economics.

Through  this  issue,  the  Journal  of  Financial  Economics  honors  its 
founder by publishing scholarly analyses of his impact on finance. The 
issue  has five articles, beginning with a  general overview  of Jensen’s 
career, followed by four articles that assess Jensen’s key scientific con-
tributions in chronological order: 

• Ren´e M. Stulz analyzes the broad sweep of Jensen’s career and dis-
cusses the nature and scale of his overall contribution to financial 
economics.

• Eugene Fama, Jensen’s Ph.D. advisor at the University of Chicago, 
and Kenneth French, focus on Jensen’s early career contributions to 
asset pricing and the efficient markets hypothesis.

• Patrick Bolton evaluates the contributions of Jensen’s most highly 
cited article –  the 1976 Journal of Financial Economics paper titled 
Theory of the firm: Managerial behavior, agency costs and ownership 
structure, co-authored with William H. Meckling – in the context of 
the evolution of corporate finance theory.

• Yueran Ma and Andrei Shleifer assess how Jensen’s work influenced 

our understanding of corporate governance.

• Harry  DeAngelo, Kathleen  Kahle, and Douglas  J. Skinner  evaluate 
the impact on the study of corporate finance of Jensen’s second most 
highly cited paper, his 1986 analysis of the agency costs of free cash 
flow.
